Frequently asked questions

Clear answers about the model

What does capacity reservation mean?

A customer contracts for a defined renewable capacity or annual energy allocation associated with generation and storage assets. CloudEnergy manages the infrastructure and digital allocation layer.

Do customers own individual solar panels?

No. Customers reserve contracted capacity or energy benefits rather than owning and operating the entire physical asset.

Can this work without rooftop installation?

Yes—that is the core proposition. Assets can be located where renewable resources and project economics are stronger, subject to applicable market and settlement rules.

Are calculator results guaranteed?

No. The calculator provides indicative planning estimates. A binding proposal requires project, tariff, regulatory, financing and customer-demand assessment.

Is CloudEnergy currently accepting payments?

No. CloudEnergy is currently developing its first projects. Early-access registration is only an expression of interest and requires no payment.

How it works

From electricity demand to reserved clean-energy capacity.

CloudEnergy coordinates project development, generation, battery storage, energy management and contractual allocation in one operating model.

01

Demand assessment

We analyse customer load, current tariff, operating hours, location and target savings to determine an indicative capacity or fixed annual-energy requirement.

02

Project matching

Demand is matched with planned or aggregated solar, wind, hybrid and BESS assets located where generation and grid economics are strongest.

03

Long-term reservation

The customer contracts for a defined allocation and term. Commercial structures may combine an upfront reservation contribution with a lower per-unit energy charge.

04

Intelligent operation

The EMS forecasts generation and demand, coordinates battery dispatch and maintains auditable records of contracted allocations.

05

Benefits and visibility

Customers receive contracted energy benefits or eligible credits and visibility into generation, allocation, savings and environmental impact.

The cost challenge

Continuous grid power is reliable—but expensive

Data centres consume electricity around the clock and generally depend on the grid for operational reliability. Their challenge is the cost of that continuous consumption and the difficulty of installing enough solar at each facility to offset it.

01

High grid expenditure

Large 24/7 compute loads create substantial monthly electricity costs and long-term tariff exposure.

02

Limited onsite space

Rooftops and nearby land are rarely sufficient to install solar capacity matching total data-centre demand.

03

Location constraints

Solar performance and available land at the facility may be weaker than at optimally located remote plants.

04

Renewable targets

Operators need measurable solar allocation and cost benefits while keeping their existing grid connection.

CloudEnergy architecture

Remote solar capacity, digitally allocated alongside grid supply.

01

Hybrid generation

Solar, wind and complementary assets can be developed or aggregated across strong-resource locations.

02

Battery storage

BESS can optimise the availability and value of solar generation without being presented as a replacement for the data centre’s grid supply.

03

Intelligent EMS

Forecasting measures generation, storage activity and each customer’s contracted digital allocation.

04

Capacity reservation

Data centres digitally reserve defined solar capacity or annual generated units from remotely located plants.

Commercial direction

Reserve solar around the compute and cost roadmap.

CloudEnergy can evaluate grid consumption, annual energy requirements, current tariff and growth phases—then size a remote solar reservation designed to reduce energy expenditure while the facility remains grid connected.
